Positioning & messaging brief

The thinking behind the landing page: who it’s for, what we say, how we’ll measure it, and what we’ll test first.

1. Positioning statement

For operations leads at multi-location retail and café businesses (2–50 sites) who lose hours and margin to spreadsheet rosters, Shiftnest is the staff-scheduling app that builds a budget-safe roster in minutes and warns you about overtime before it happens. Unlike spreadsheets or generic HR suites, Shiftnest is built around labour cost against forecast sales, store by store.

4. Messaging house

Every store fully staffed, on budget, without the Sunday-night spreadsheet.

Time back

Auto-fill from availability and skills; copy last week; one-tap approvals.

Proof to build: median time-to-publish per roster (product analytics).

Margin protected

Live labour % vs forecast sales; overtime and break-rule warnings before publish.

Proof to build: overtime hours before/after in pilot stores vs matched control stores.

Happier teams

Mobile roster, self-serve swaps, fewer last-minute changes.

Proof to build: swap fill rate, staff app rating, no-show rate.

Tone: practical, warm, specific numbers over adjectives. Never blame managers; blame the spreadsheet.

5. Landing page structure (and why)

  1. Hero: one job-to-be-done headline (A/B tested by angle), product mock showing the overtime warning, free-plan CTA to reduce friction.
  2. Problem: three pains in the buyer’s words, so they self-qualify.
  3. How it works + role tabs: speaks to each member of the buying group without a long page.
  4. Plan picker: recommends the smallest plan that fits; regional price points; annual toggle anchors the discount.
  5. ROI estimator: gives the champion a number to forward to finance; assumptions are editable to build trust.
  6. Comparison, testimonials, FAQ: objection handling.
  7. Demo form: short, validated, scores the lead and routes to sales-assist, guided trial or self-serve.
